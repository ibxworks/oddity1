The Intelligence Illusion

Essay · 2024 · Annotation layer active

Artificial intelligence does not think. It predicts. The distinction sounds academic until you realize that every confident answer an AI gives you is, at its core, a sophisticated guess — a pattern matched against a vast corpus of human output, with no ground truth in sight. This is not a flaw. It is the architecture.

We have built these systems to be fluent, and fluency, it turns out, is a nearly perfect simulation of understanding. A student who has read enough essays can reproduce the conventions of argument without grasping the argument itself. Large language models have read more essays than any student ever will.

The problem compounds when we consider what we actually do with AI output. Most users treat a well-formed sentence as evidence of a well-formed idea. Grammar becomes epistemology. Coherence becomes proof. We have been trained by centuries of text — by books, journalism, scholarship — to trust the written form, and AI exploits that trust with extraordinary efficiency.

There is a strange recursion at work here: the models were trained on human thought, and now human thought is being shaped by the models. The feedback loop is not hypothetical. It is happening in classrooms, newsrooms, and boardrooms right now. We outsource the first draft, then we edit it, and gradually the first draft becomes the thought.

None of this means AI is useless — far from it. But usefulness is not the same as intelligence, and efficiency is not the same as understanding. The danger is not that machines will start to think. The danger is that we will stop.
